Driver/Operator Academy

A structured path from apparatus driving fundamentals to pumper operations. Learn the concept, perform a station drill, use FireOps Calc to practice the numbers, and finish with randomized practice exams.

How FireOps names the tracks: DO1 covers driver/operator foundations and practical driving skills. DO2 covers pumper, hydraulics, water supply and advanced pump operations. These are FireOps learning-track names; your AHJ or state may use Driver Operator, Driver Operator Pumper, Driver Operator Aerial, or other titles.

Academy system

More than a study guide

FireOps Calc already has the calculators and training tools. The academy turns them into a sequence a student can actually follow.

1 · Learn

Short lessons

Plain-language concepts built around decisions an engineer must make.

2 · Drill

Hands-on practice

Every module includes a practical station evolution or checkoff.

3 · Apply

Live tools

Hydraulics modules link directly to the PDP, friction-loss, hydrant, relay, drafting and standpipe tools.

4 · Test

Practice exams

Randomized exams and local progress storage help students identify weak areas.

Recommended sequence

From new driver to pump operator

Phase 1

DO1 Academy

Complete all driving and apparatus modules. Perform a full cone-course mock test and route drive with your department.

Phase 2

DO2 Academy

Work through pump theory and math, then perform progressive water-flow evolutions with an instructor.

Phase 3

Scenario repetition

Use FireOps scenarios, 1410-style evolutions, flashcards and the calculator until the calculations and operating sequence are automatic.

Standards note: NFPA now places fire apparatus driver/operator professional qualifications within NFPA 1010 (2024). Colorado reference documents available online include older NFPA 1002-based practical packets and testing policies. FireOps Calc intentionally teaches the durable competencies—inspection, driving, pump operations, water supply and safe decision-making—while directing students to verify the current AHJ/state testing requirements before certification.
