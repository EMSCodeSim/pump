Hand method for friction loss and pump calculations.

The firefighter hand method is a five-finger memory aid for pump discharge pressure (PDP). Each finger represents one part of the pump calculation: nozzle pressure, friction loss, elevation, appliance loss, and the final pump discharge pressure.

What is the firefighter hand method?

The hand method is a driver/operator teaching shortcut for building a pump discharge pressure calculation in the correct order. It does not replace the underlying math. Instead, each finger reminds the operator which pressure component comes next.

PDP = NP + FL ± Elevation + AL

How the hand method works for friction loss

Friction loss is the index-finger step. After identifying nozzle pressure, determine the friction loss for the actual hose size, hose length, and flow. That value can come from your department pump card, hose testing data, a friction loss formula, or the FireOps Calc friction loss calculator. Then add it to nozzle pressure before accounting for elevation and appliance loss.

Important: the hand method does not assign one universal friction-loss number. A 1¾-inch attack line flowing 150 GPM has a different loss than the same line flowing 185 GPM, and department hose characteristics can differ. Use the data your department trains with.

Step-by-step pump calculation

Start with nozzle pressure.Use the pressure required by the nozzle your crew is actually operating.
Add friction loss.Use hose size, length, and flow with your department data, chart, or calculator.
Add or subtract elevation.A common training rule is 5 psi for each 10 feet of elevation, but follow department practice.
Add appliance loss when needed.Account for wyes, manifolds, master-stream devices, or other appliances when required.
Total the PDP.Set the calculated pressure, communicate with the crew, and verify line performance.

Hand method example

Training setup: 200 feet of 1¾-inch hose, fog nozzle flowing 185 GPM at 50 psi, 30 psi friction loss, 20 feet of elevation, and a wye requiring 10 psi appliance loss.

Thumb: Nozzle Pressure50 psi
Index: Friction Loss30 psi
Middle: Elevation+10 psi
Ring: Appliance Loss+10 psi
Pinky: Pump Discharge Pressure100 psi

Training answer: 100 psi PDP. In operations, use your department hose/nozzle data and SOP.

Common mistakes

Guessing friction loss

Use a known chart, hose data, formula, or calculator instead of memorizing one number for every line.

Wrong nozzle pressure

Do not assume every fog nozzle or smooth bore uses the same pressure.

Forgetting elevation

Above-grade and below-grade operations change the required pump pressure.

No field verification

The calculation is the starting point. Confirm actual stream and crew feedback.

Hand method FAQ

What is the hand method for friction loss?

Friction loss is the index-finger step in the firefighter hand method. Determine the actual hose friction loss for the selected hose, length, and flow, then add it to nozzle pressure.

What is the firefighter hand method formula?

PDP = NP + FL ± elevation + appliance loss. The hand method simply helps students remember that order.

Does the hand method replace a friction loss calculation?

No. It tells you where friction loss belongs in the pump calculation. You still need to calculate or look up the correct friction-loss value.

Does this replace department SOPs?

No. This page is for training and review. Always follow department pump procedures, hose/nozzle data, and instructor guidance.
